📜 The Commissioner's Chronicle — Chapter III: THE SCHISM

Nathan had not won a battle.

And somehow, that made everything worse.

For two weeks, whispers crawled beneath the doors of the Twelve Houses: Baba Yaga is weak. The champion bleeds. The throne is empty even while he sits upon it.

Eimak had expected rebellion.

He had not expected two rebellions.

THE ORDER OF THE SEVERED CROWN followed the old prophecy. Nathan must be allowed to crawl back toward power. Let him believe the realm has forgiven him. Let him approach the playoffs wearing his crown once more. Only then would the final humiliation mean anything.

THE BOOGER LIBERATION FRONT had no patience for theater. Nathan was 0–2. They wanted 0–3. Then 0–4. They wanted Baba Yaga dragged through the mud until there was no king left to execute.

For the first time, the revolutionaries turned their weapons toward one another.

Then Roy arrived.

Eimak stared at Josh Allen. Jahmyr Gibbs. Chris Olave. Davante Adams. Travis Kelce.

“How has one man assembled this much talent and still made me hate looking at it?”

Roy's roster was a perfectly cooked steak floating in a gas-station toilet: obviously valuable, completely ruined by the circumstances surrounding it.

Eimak carved Roy's victory into the stone, gagged, and washed the knife twice.

But the Temple fell silent when Nathan entered.

Across the chamber stood Sawyer — undefeated.

The Liberation Front screamed for Nathan's third defeat.

The Severed Crown drew steel.

“A king cannot be executed as a peasant.”

And then Eimak spoke the prophecy nobody wanted to hear:

Nathan would win.

Some called it betrayal. Others called it destiny. Nathan called it Week 3.

Eimak called it something else.

The beginning.

Because revolutions do not become dangerous when everyone hates the king.

They become dangerous when everyone hates the king — and begins arguing over what should happen to him.
