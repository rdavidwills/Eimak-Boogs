📜 The Commissioner's Chronicle — Chapter IV: THE HAUNTING

The king had survived.

Nathan Glueck stood again.

Not proudly. Not convincingly. But technically upright.

And in the Holy Realm, technically upright was enough.

The victory should have united the rebellion.

Instead, it destroyed what remained of it.

THE ORDER OF THE SEVERED CROWN celebrated.

Their prophecy required Nathan alive.

Let the champion recover. Let Baba Yaga crawl back toward relevance. Let Nathan begin believing the crown still belonged to him.

Then, near the playoffs, remove it.

THE BOOGER LIBERATION FRONT called this insanity.

They had wanted Nathan buried at 0–3.

Now he was 1–2.

Another victory could make him 2–2.

Two victories could make him confident.

And nothing terrified the Liberation Front more than a confident Nathan.

Arguments became threats. Threats became alliances. Alliances lasted approximately eleven minutes.

And while the revolution consumed itself, two undefeated armies quietly grew stronger.

Jacob Thomas stood at 3–0 beneath the banner of Demon Worship.

Roy Fontenelle also stood at 3–0.

Roy immediately requested that the historical record acknowledge this achievement.

The historical record responded:

Championships — 0.

Roy demanded a recount.

The recount produced zero again.

A second recount was ordered.

Still zero.

Eimak eventually instructed the scribes to stop counting because they were simply staring at an empty shelf.

But then something happened that made everyone forget about Roy.

For one beautiful moment.

A noise came from beyond the Temple walls.

Not footsteps.

Not breathing.

Something wetter.

Something was approaching.

The oldest and least reliable scriptures tell of a man named Michael Landry.

According to these deeply questionable texts, Michael had once been portrayed as a furious red-hatted MAGA disciple, a fictional Trump-loving warrior screaming his beliefs into the ancient GroupMe scrolls.

But something changed.

First Michael abandoned certainty.

Then allegiance.

Then ideology.

Then apparently the basic requirement of maintaining a recognizable identity.

His opinions folded into other opinions. His beliefs dissolved into questions. His questions dissolved into noises.

And eventually...

Michael dissolved.

No Republican. No Democrat. No revolutionary. No loyalist.

Not even really Michael anymore.

Only an enormous, shimmering, amorphous blob of political and spiritual uncertainty.

The creature floated over the Temple.

Eimak looked upward.

The blob looked downward.

Or at least Eimak assumed it did. It no longer appeared to possess eyes.

Then the thing moved.

Past Nathan.

Past Jacob.

Past Roy's empty trophy cabinet.

It drifted toward the houses of those who had lost.

And from somewhere inside its gelatinous body came a whisper:

“I don't really know what I believe anymore.”

The torches went out.

Eimak finally understood.

The revolution was no longer the only danger in the realm.

Beginning in Week Four, the losers would be haunted.
